How to structure a page so AI systems can cite it

A page becomes easier for AI systems to cite when it opens with a direct answer, uses semantic headings, contains verifiable details, and provides enough context for a quote to stand alone. This fixture guide states that rule in the first paragraph because answer-first delivery is a core part of the audit rubric. The rest of the page expands on the idea using predictable sections and stable wording that should remain unchanged across test runs.

Direct answers come first

Search systems and AI assistants often extract the earliest passage that cleanly answers a user question. When a page delays the answer behind branding or filler, the odds of extraction drop. This fixture therefore leads with a clear definition and repeats the concept in supporting paragraphs. The goal is not literary flair. The goal is stable retrieval quality that can be measured consistently across script revisions and future eval harnesses.

Evidence and structure support quoting

Structured passages make quoting easier because the model can isolate a short unit without carrying along excess context. Evidence also matters. In a real article that means dates, measurements, named sources, and tables. In this benchmark it means explicit mentions of metadata, schema, headings, dates, and internal links. Those are the deterministic clues the current script can verify today, and they are the same signals a later rubric evaluator can inspect when scoring answer readiness and structural clarity.

The guide also includes an author name and publication dates because freshness and attribution are part of the broader AEO story. Even though the deterministic script only uses those fields in heuristics, keeping them in the fixture gives the repo realistic content to evaluate. That keeps the benchmark useful for both the current script and the next layer of evals that will judge report quality or answer synthesis against saved crawl outputs.
