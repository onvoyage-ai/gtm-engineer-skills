Fixture product page with machine-readable product signals

Product pages need to explain what the product does, who it is for, and why it matters in language that an AI system can quote without surrounding context. This fixture page exists to satisfy that standard. It contains a clear title, long description, canonical URL, Open Graph fields, and Product schema so the audit can verify that all of those machine-readable signals are present at once.

What this benchmark product represents

The product itself is fictional. Its job is to simulate a real SaaS page with stable semantics and enough body text to clear the depth threshold. It also links heavily to related resources because internal linking is part of the deterministic score. The wording intentionally references discoverability, answers, and citations so the page aligns with the larger repository theme instead of looking like generic lorem ipsum. That makes failures easier to interpret when contributors inspect the saved JSON report from the test run.

Why regression fixtures matter

Without fixtures, contributors would have to depend on external websites that can change structure, metadata, or crawl availability at any time. Regression tests would then fail noisily for reasons unrelated to code quality. By keeping the product page local, the suite can assert a stable foundational score and a stable set of passing checks. That is the right first milestone for this repo because it creates confidence in the deterministic layer before adding more expensive rubric-based or agent-based evaluation lanes.

The final paragraph exists mainly to push the content above the minimum word count while preserving readable prose. Real product pages should of course include better proof, screenshots, and deeper buyer-facing content. For a test asset, the goal is simpler: keep enough structure and text in place that the crawler sees a healthy, well-linked, machine-readable page every time the test suite runs locally or in CI.
